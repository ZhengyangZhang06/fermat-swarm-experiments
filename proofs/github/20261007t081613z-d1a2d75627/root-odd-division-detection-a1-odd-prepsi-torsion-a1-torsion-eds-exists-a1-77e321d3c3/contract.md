## Theorem `Submission.p03_torsion_eds_exists_68cf3476_d2`

Let k be an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field with decidable equality, and let W be a Weierstrass equation over k with W.Δ ≠ 0. Let A=k[X,Y]/(W(X,Y)), let q:k[X,Y]→A be the quotient map, let h=q(W.ψ₂), and let I_(x,y) be the ideal of A generated by the classes of X−x and Y−y. There exists f:ℕ→A with f₀=0, f₁=1, f₂=h, f₃=q(C(W.Ψ₃)), f₄=h q(C(W.preΨ₄)); for every r≥2, f_(2r+1)=f_(r+2)f_r³−f_(r−1)f_(r+1)³; for every r≥3, h f_(2r)=f_r(f_(r+2)f_(r−1)²−f_(r−2)f_(r+1)²); and for every n>0 and every nonsingular affine point P=(x,y), f_n∈I_(x,y) if and only if nP=O. Here C includes a univariate polynomial in k[X] into k[X,Y], and O denotes the identity in W.toAffine.Point.

## Proof

1. Let k, W and the nonzero discriminant satisfy the hypotheses. Put A=k[X,Y]/(W(X,Y)), let q be its quotient homomorphism, and identify A with the affine coordinate ring of the smooth projective Weierstrass cubic E minus its identity O. Its fraction field is the rational function field k(E). Write x=q(Polynomial.C Polynomial.X), y=q(Polynomial.X), and h=2y+a₁x+a₃=q(W.ψ₂). Smoothness and geometric integrality follow from the nonzero discriminant. The group E(k), with its chord-and-tangent law and identity O, is the group W.toAffine.Point used in the statement. We use the following standard facts under these hypotheses: principal divisors have degree zero; a rational function with zero divisor on a smooth projective integral curve over an algebraically closed field is constant; and a nonconstant morphism of such curves is finite and surjective, its fiber multiplicities summing to its function-field degree. These are Silverman, The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, second edition, Chapter II, §§2–3. The regular group law, line intersection rule, and local parameters of a smooth Weierstrass cubic are treated in Chapter III, §§2–4 and Chapter IV, §1.
2. At O put t=−x/y and s=−1/y. The equation is s=t³+a₁ts+a₂t²s+a₃s²+a₄ts²+a₆s³. The derivative in s of the left side minus the right side equals 1 at (0,0). Thus t is a uniformizer and the equation recursively determines s=t³+terms of higher degree. Hence x=t⁻²+terms of higher order and y=−t⁻³+terms of higher order. The group law in t has linear part t₁+t₂, since setting either argument equal to O gives the other argument. For every positive integer m, t∘[m]=mt+terms of higher degree. Characteristic zero makes m nonzero. Translation conjugates the local computation at O to that at any point, so [m] is everywhere unramified. It is nonconstant and hence finite and surjective. If d_m is its degree, every fiber, in particular E[m], has exactly d_m distinct points.
3. Set G=4x³+b₂x²+2b₄x+b₆ and z=h/2. Completion of the square gives z²=G/4. The three roots of G in k are distinct: a repeated root would give a singular point (x,z)=(root,0). The affine two-torsion points are exactly these three points, because negation fixes (x,y) exactly when h=0. Near each of them z is a uniformizer and x−x(T) has order two, with nonzero quadratic coefficient. Away from these points, at affine points, x is a local parameter after subtracting its value, because the derivative of the equation in y is h≠0.
4. For integers m>r≥1 put H=x∘[m]−x∘[r]. At O its leading term is (m⁻²−r⁻²)t⁻², which is nonzero. At a source point P with both images affine, a zero occurs exactly when [m]P=[r]P or [m]P=−[r]P. If their common image up to sign is not two-torsion, translate the source by P and use the local parameter from step 2. In the equal-image case the nonzero linear coefficient of the x-coordinate is multiplied by m−r; in the opposite-image case, use x(Q)=x(−Q), giving m+r. Both yield simple zeros. If both images equal the same nonzero two-torsion point T, the quadratic expansion from step 3 gives leading coefficient c(m²−r²) with c≠0, hence order two. If exactly one image is O, H has a pole of order two. If both are O, the translated source expansion has leading coefficient m⁻²−r⁻² and again order −2. At every other point H is regular and nonzero. Since all pullbacks of O have multiplicity one, these cases prove div(H)=[m+r]⁎(O)+[m−r]⁎(O)−2[m]⁎(O)−2[r]⁎(O).
5. Taking degrees gives d_(m+r)+d_(m−r)=2d_m+2d_r. Here d₁=1, while step 3 gives four two-torsion points including O, so d₂=4. Taking r=1 yields d_(m+1)=2d_m+2−d_(m−1) for m≥2. Induction gives d_m=m², since 2m²+2−(m−1)²=(m+1)².
6. For any points P,Q, the divisor (P)+(Q)−(P+Q)−(O) is principal. If either point is O it is zero. If P+Q=O, the vertical line through P gives it, including the two-torsion tangent case. Otherwise the secant or tangent through P,Q has third intersection −(P+Q); divide its line function by the vertical line through P+Q. Their intersection divisors, with multiplicities, give the claimed divisor. Iterating this relation shows that Σ(T)−N(O) is linearly equivalent to (ΣT)−(O) for any list of N points. The group sum of E[m] is O: pair each point with its negative. For odd m the only unpaired point is O, since a point killed by both 2 and m is zero by Bézout. For even m the unpaired points are exactly E[2]; their three nonzero points lie on the line h=0 and sum to O by the line intersection rule. Therefore D_m=Σ_(T∈E[m])(T)−m²(O) is principal.
7. Choose its rational function f_m with leading term (−1)^(m−1)m t^(−(m²−1)) at O. The order of D_m at O is 1−m², and the chosen leading coefficient is nonzero, so multiplication by a constant gives this normalization. The function is unique, since two choices have constant quotient with leading coefficient 1. In particular f₁=1; set f₀=0. For m≥1 all affine orders of f_m are nonnegative, so f_m is regular on E minus O and belongs to A. More explicitly, smooth affine local rings are discrete valuation rings, so nonnegative order gives membership in every local ring at a closed point. Closed points correspond to maximal ideals of A over the algebraically closed field k. An element u of Frac(A) lying in all A_M belongs to A: otherwise the denominator ideal {a∈A : au∈A} is proper, is contained in a maximal ideal M, and contradicts a denominator outside M furnished by u∈A_M. This justifies viewing the whole chosen sequence as a function ℕ→A.
8. Comparing D_(m+r)+D_(m−r)−2D_m−2D_r with step 4 shows that f_(m+r)f_(m−r)/(f_m²f_r²) and H have equal divisors. Their leading coefficients at O are respectively (m²−r²)/(m²r²) and its negative. Their constant quotient is therefore −1, giving H=−f_(m+r)f_(m−r)/(f_m²f_r²). With r=1 this gives x∘[m]=x−f_(m+1)f_(m−1)/f_m² for m≥2. For m=1 the same identity follows from f₀=0 and f₁=1. All denominators are nonzero rational functions; no evaluation at a denominator zero is used.
9. At the three nonzero two-torsion points, h=2z has order one by step 3; it has no other affine zeros. At O its leading term is −2t⁻³. Thus its divisor and normalization give f₂=h. Put B=3x²+2a₂x+a₄−a₁y and C=6x²+b₂x+b₄. The defining equation and coefficients give h²=G, 2B+a₁h=C, and b₂b₆−b₄²=4b₈, where b₂=a₁²+4a₂, b₄=2a₄+a₁a₃, b₆=a₃²+4a₆, and b₈=a₁²a₆+4a₂a₆−a₁a₃a₄+a₂a₃²−a₄². The tangent slope B/h yields x∘[2]=(B/h)²+a₁B/h−a₂−2x. Comparing with step 8 gives f₃=(3x+a₂)G−B²−a₁Bh=(3x+b₂/4)G−C²/4. Expansion gives 3x⁴+b₂x³+3b₄x²+3b₆x+(b₂b₆−b₄²)/4, which equals q(Polynomial.C W.Ψ₃).
10. The divisor of (h∘[2])h⁴ is [4]⁎(O)−16(O), by pulling back div(h)=[2]⁎(O)−4(O). Its leading term is (−2(2t)⁻³)(−2t⁻³)⁴=−4t⁻¹⁵, so normalization gives f₄=(h∘[2])h⁴. Put N=B²+a₁Bh−(a₂+2x)h², so x∘[2]=N/h². The tangent and negation formulas give (h∘[2])h³=−(2B+a₁h)N+(2Bx−(2y+a₃)h)h². Using N=C²/4−(b₂/4+2x)G, 2Bx−(2y+a₃)h=Cx−G, and the expression for f₃ gives (h∘[2])h³=Cf₃−G². Its expansion is 2x⁶+b₂x⁵+5b₄x⁴+10b₆x³+(6b₈+b₂b₆−b₄²)x²+(b₂b₈−b₄b₆)x+b₄b₈−b₆². The relation b₂b₆−b₄²=4b₈ makes the x² coefficient 10b₈. Thus Cf₃−G² is precisely J=W.preΨ₄(x), and f₄=h q(Polynomial.C W.preΨ₄).
11. Subtract the two multiplication-coordinate identities in step 8 and compare with its formula for H. Clearing nonzero rational-function denominators gives f_(m+r)f_(m−r)=f_(m+1)f_(m−1)f_r²−f_(r+1)f_(r−1)f_m² for m>r≥1. Set m=r+1 to obtain f_(2r+1)=f_(r+2)f_r³−f_(r−1)f_(r+1)³ for r≥2. For r≥3 use m=r+1 and the smaller index r−1 to obtain h f_(2r)=f_r(f_(r+2)f_(r−1)²−f_(r−2)f_(r+1)²). These equalities in k(E) are equalities in A because A embeds in its fraction field.
12. Fix n>0 and a nonsingular affine point P=(x₀,y₀). Its coefficient in D_n is 1 if nP=O and 0 otherwise. A regular function in its discrete valuation local ring vanishes in the residue field precisely when its order is positive. Evaluation at P on A has kernel the ideal generated by x−x₀ and y−y₀: quotienting by those generators substitutes the coordinates and gives k, since W(x₀,y₀)=0. This is exactly CoordinateRing.XYIdeal W.toAffine x₀ (Polynomial.C y₀). Therefore f_n belongs to that ideal exactly when nP=O. Together with steps 7, 9, 10, and 11 this proves every conjunct of the asserted existence statement.

## Key steps

1. Use the local parameter t=−x/y to prove multiplication maps are finite, surjective, and unramified.
2. Compute the divisor of x∘[m]−x∘[r], including two-torsion zeros and common poles.
3. Deduce deg([m])=m² from the divisor relation and the four two-torsion points.
4. Use line divisors and the zero group sum of E[m] to construct normalized functions with torsion divisors.
5. Show the functions have no affine poles and therefore belong to the coordinate ring.
6. Compare divisors and leading terms to obtain multiplication-coordinate identities.
7. Compute the exact initial functions f₂, f₃, and f₄ by tangent formulas.
8. Derive both elliptic recurrences by subtracting multiplication-coordinate identities.
9. Translate affine divisor orders into membership in the point ideal.
