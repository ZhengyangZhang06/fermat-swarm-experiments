## Theorem `Submission.p03_tu_bilateral_summable_68cf3476`

Let F be a complete normed field and Ω a normed field equipped with a NormedAlgebra F Ω structure and algebraic over F. Assume the norm of Ω satisfies ‖x+y‖ ≤ max(‖x‖,‖y‖). Let q ∈ F satisfy 0 < ‖q‖ < 1, and put Q = algebraMap F Ω q. For every unit u ∈ Ωˣ such that u ≠ Q^m for every integer m, both ℤ-indexed families Q^n u/(1−Q^n u)^2 and (Q^n u)^2/(1−Q^n u)^3 are summable in Ω. Completeness of Ω is not assumed.

## Proof

1. Put R = ‖q‖ and U = ‖u‖. Then 0 < R < 1 and U > 0. NormedAlgebra makes the inclusion F → Ω norm-preserving, so ‖Q‖ = R and Q ≠ 0. The element q⁻¹ has norm R⁻¹ > 1; hence F is nontrivially normed, with its original norm and field operations.

2. Let E = F(u) be the intermediate field of Ω generated by u. Algebraicity of Ω/F makes u algebraic, and therefore E is finite-dimensional over F. Give E its norm and field operations induced from Ω. Its inclusion into Ω is isometric, and its scalar action from F is compatible with the norm. The pinned theorem FiniteDimensional.complete applies because F is complete and nontrivially normed, so E is complete. The elements Q, u and every summand under consideration belong to E.

3. In an ultrametric normed field, if ‖a‖ > ‖b‖, then ‖a−b‖ = ‖a‖: one inequality is ultrametric, and a = (a−b)+b rules out a strictly smaller norm for a−b. Also 1−Q^n u never vanishes. Indeed Q^n u = 1 would imply u = Q^(−n), contrary to the hypothesis.

4. For nonnegative indices n = j, the quantity z_j = Q^j u has norm R^j U, which tends to zero. For all sufficiently large j it is less than one. Step 3 then gives ‖1−z_j‖ = 1. Consequently the first summand has norm R^j U, and the second has norm (R^j U)^2 ≤ R^j U. The real family U R^j is summable. Comparison in the complete field E, with finitely many initial exceptions, proves summability of both nonnegative-index families.

5. Write the negative indices as n = −(j+1), where j ∈ ℕ. Now z_j = Q^(−(j+1))u has norm U/R^(j+1), which eventually exceeds one. Step 3 gives ‖1−z_j‖ = ‖z_j‖. Both rational summands consequently have norm ‖z_j‖⁻¹ = R^(j+1)/U. This is a summable real geometric family. Comparison in E, again allowing finitely many initial exceptions, proves summability of both negative-index families.

6. The integers are the disjoint union of j ↦ j and j ↦ −(j+1). Combining the two summable families from steps 4 and 5 therefore proves unordered summability over ℤ in E. Finally apply the continuous additive inclusion E → Ω to their convergent nets of finite partial sums. The resulting families are exactly those in the statement, proving summability in Ω without requiring Ω to be complete.

## Key steps

1. Use q to obtain nontriviality of the base-field norm.
2. Place all summands in the finite complete intermediate field F(u).
3. Exclude zero denominators and establish the unequal-norm subtraction rule.
4. Bound the nonnegative tails by U‖q‖^j.
5. Bound the negative tails by ‖q‖^(j+1)/U.
6. Combine the two integer halves and map their sums into Ω.
