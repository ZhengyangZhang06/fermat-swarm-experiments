## Theorem `Submission.p05_fr_rhm_presentation_ideals_eq_a5b449214a`

Let R be a commutative ring and M an R-module whose additive group is commutative. Let n,p,n′,p′ be natural numbers, P an n-by-p matrix and Q an n′-by-p′ matrix over R, and let π:R^n→M and π′:R^{n′}→M be surjective R-linear maps with ker(π)=im(P) and ker(π′)=im(Q). For every natural number r, the ideal generated by the (n−r)-minors of P equals the ideal generated by the (n′−r)-minors of Q. Subtraction is truncated at zero; minors use injective row and column selections, the empty determinant is 1, and an unavailable positive size generates the zero ideal.

## Proof

1. For a finite matrix B write D_s(B) for the ideal generated by its s-by-s minors with injective row and column selections. In particular D_0(B)=R, since both selections from the empty set exist and the empty determinant is 1. If the size is unavailable, the generating set is empty and its ideal is zero.
2. Suppose the columns of B are linear combinations of the columns of C, with the same row set. For any selected s rows and s columns of B, expand the determinant multilinearly in its columns. Each term is a coefficient times the determinant of s selected columns of C on those rows. If any columns repeat, alternation makes the determinant zero; otherwise those columns constitute an injective selection and give a generator of D_s(C). Thus D_s(B)⊆D_s(C). For s=0 both ideals are R. Consequently two finite column lists generating the same submodule give equal ideals. The identical expansion in rows shows D_s(UB)⊆D_s(B) for any compatible square matrix U; when U is invertible, applying the inclusion to U⁻¹ proves equality. The column argument proves invariance under invertible column operations as well.
3. Expanding an (s+1)-minor along one row writes it as a sum of entries times s-minors. Hence D_(s+1)(B)⊆D_s(B), including s=0. If there are no (s+1)-minors the inclusion is automatic.
4. Let m_1,…,m_N generate M and let B be a finite matrix whose columns generate all their relations. Adjoin a redundant generator y=Σ_i a_i m_i. The new relation module is generated by the old columns with last coordinate zero and by v=(−a_1,…,−a_N,1): for any relation (b,c), subtract c v, leaving the old relation (b+c a,0). The resulting relation matrix is [[B,−a],[0,1]]. Adding a_i times its last row to row i transforms it into diag(B,1); this row transformation is invertible, with inverse obtained by subtraction.
5. Fix r<N and put s=N−r≥1. In diag(B,1), an (s+1)-minor using both the new row and new column is, up to sign, an s-minor of B, and every s-minor occurs this way. A minor using exactly one of the new row and new column has a zero row or column. A minor using neither is an (s+1)-minor of B and belongs to D_s(B) by step 3. These statements, together with step 2, prove that adjoining y preserves D_(N−r)(B), with the size increased to (N+1)−r in the new presentation. For r=N the old size is zero and the new 1-minors include 1. For r>N both truncated sizes are zero. Thus adjoining a redundant generator preserves the ideal for every r, including N=0.
6. For the supplied presentation π, take the generators π(e_i) and relation columns P; the kernel hypothesis says precisely that these are all the relations. Do the same for π′ and Q. Adjoin the generators from π′ to the first list and those from π to the second list. Surjectivity expresses each added generator as a linear combination of the original list, so repeated application of steps 4–5 is valid. The two enlarged lists differ only by a permutation. Permuting the corresponding coordinates is an invertible row operation. After this permutation the two finite relation lists generate the same kernel, so step 2 equates their minor ideals. Undoing the equalities from the redundant-generator additions proves the claimed equality for the original presentations and the given r.

## Key steps

1. Establish minor-ideal containment by multilinear column and row expansion.
2. Derive invariance under changing relation generators and invertible row operations.
3. Use cofactor expansion to compare consecutive minor sizes.
4. Describe the kernel after adding a redundant module generator and reduce its matrix to diag(B,1).
5. Check all truncated-size cases and compare the two presentations through their combined generating list.
