## Theorem `Submission.p05_hte_finite_tensor_zero_witness_a5b449214a`

Let k and D be commutative rings, with D a k-algebra, and let M and P be D-modules whose additive structures are commutative groups. If m⊗_D p=0, there are finite sets S⊆D and F⊆M, with m∈F, such that the following holds. For every k-subalgebra A⊆D containing S and every A-submodule N⊆M containing F, the pure tensor of m, regarded as an element of N, with p is zero in N⊗_A P. All A-actions are restrictions of the given D-actions.

## Proof

1. Let G be the free abelian group on M×P, writing [u,z] for a basis generator. Let R be the subgroup generated by the zero relations [0,z] and [u,0], the additivity relations [u+v,z]−[u,z]−[v,z] and [u,z+w]−[u,z]−[u,w], and the balancing relations [d•u,z]−[u,d•z], for all indicated elements and d∈D.
2. The quotient G/R is the usual tensor product as an additive group. For completeness, d•[u,z]=[d•u,z] preserves R: it sends additivity and zero relations to relations, and sends a balancing relation with scalar e to a balancing relation with scalar e after using d•(e•u)=e•(d•u). The module laws on the quotient follow from the D-module laws on M and the first-coordinate additivity relations. The map (u,z)↦[u,z] modulo R is D-bilinear, because balancing also gives [u,d•z]=[d•u,z]. It induces a map from the pinned tensor product to G/R. Conversely, [u,z]↦u⊗_D z defines an additive map from G/R to the tensor product since every displayed relation vanishes there. These maps are inverse on pair generators, which generate both objects. This also agrees directly with the pinned free-additive-monoid presentation: its zero, addition, balancing and commutativity relations hold in G/R, and its tensor product is an additive group over the ring D.
3. The hypothesis therefore says [m,p]∈R. The elements that are finite integer linear combinations of the displayed generators form a subgroup: zero is the empty combination, sums concatenate combinations, and negatives negate coefficients. This subgroup contains all generators and is contained in every subgroup containing them. Hence it is R, and [m,p] has an expression as a finite integer linear combination of displayed relations.
4. Let S contain all balancing scalars in this finite expression. Let F contain m and every first coordinate of every pair occurring in its relations; in particular include all first-coordinate operands, their sums, zero where it occurs, and the products d•u in balancing relations. These are finite sets. Fix a k-subalgebra A containing S and an A-submodule N of M containing F. Restrict the D-actions on M and P to A.
5. Lift every pair in the chosen expression to N×P by giving its first coordinate the known membership proof in N. Each lifted zero or additivity relation is a relation for N⊗_A P, since N inherits the addition and zero of M. Each lifted balancing relation uses a scalar d in S⊆A; regarded as an element of A, this scalar acts by its original D-action, so the relation is also a valid A-balancing relation. Equalities among first coordinates lift to N because subtype inclusion is injective.
6. The induced map from the free abelian group on N×P to G is injective: the inclusion of pairs is injective, so distinct basis generators have distinct images, and a finite integer coefficient function can map to zero only if all its coefficients vanish. Both the lifted [m,p] and the lifted finite sum map to the original formal-sum identity from step 3. Injectivity proves that identity already in the free abelian group on N×P. Passing to its tensor-product quotient annihilates all lifted relations, giving m⊗_A p=0 in N⊗_A P. The subtype element is independent of the chosen proof hm:m∈N, so the result holds for every such hm, as asserted.

## Key steps

1. Present the tensor product as a free abelian group modulo zero, additivity and balancing relations.
2. Express zero membership by a finite integer combination of defining relations.
3. Collect the finitely many scalars and first coordinates in the witness.
4. Lift the witness to any subalgebra and submodule containing those supports.
5. Use injectivity on free abelian groups, then pass to the smaller tensor quotient.
