## Theorem `Submission.p05_hopf_tensor_equalizer_a5b449214a`

Let k be a field, H a commutative Hopf k-algebra, and K a k-subalgebra such that Δ(K) lies in the k-linear span of tensors with both factors in K and S(K)⊆K. Give H its K-module structure by inclusion. For every x∈H, the equality x⊗_K1=1⊗_Kx in H⊗_K H implies x∈K.

## Proof

1. Every subalgebra D⊂H satisfying the two closure conditions inherits a compatible commutative Hopf structure. Extend a basis of D to a basis of H to obtain a k-linear retraction H→D. Its tensor powers make the double and triple tensor inclusions injective. The double inclusion has image exactly the span of tensors with both factors in D. Thus Δ restricts uniquely to D. Double injectivity transfers multiplication and unit preservation; triple injectivity transfers coassociativity. Restrict ε and, using antipode stability, S. Their identities hold after the injective inclusion in H and hence in D. In particular this applies to K.
2. Every finite subset E⊂H lies in a finite-dimensional subspace V containing 1 with Δ(V)⊂V⊗_k H. For f∈E∪{1}, write Δ(f)=Σ_i v_i⊗w_i with independent w_i by collecting second factors in a basis of their finite span. Coassociativity gives Σ_i Δ(v_i)⊗w_i=Σ_i v_i⊗Δ(w_i). Extend the coordinate functionals of the w_i to H by basis extension and apply them to the third factor. This gives Δ(v_i)∈span_k{v_j}⊗H. The right counit identity gives f=Σ_i ε(w_i)v_i. Summing these finite-dimensional stable spans produces V.
3. For a commutative ring D and D-modules C and P, construct C⊗_D P from the free abelian group on pairs (c,p), quotienting by the subgroup generated by additivity in both coordinates and balancing relations (dc,p)−(c,dp). Multiplication on the first coordinate preserves the relation subgroup, giving a D-module. Balanced biadditive maps descend uniquely, so the quotient has the tensor-product universal property. The subgroup generated by the relations consists exactly of finite integer linear combinations of them: such combinations form a subgroup containing the generators and lie in every subgroup containing them. Thus an equality to zero in a tensor product has a finite relation witness. This construction agrees with the pinned tensor product: its zero, addition, balancing and commutativity relations hold here, and additivity over rings gives additive inverses there; the maps fixing pair generators are inverse.
4. First let D⊂H satisfy both Hopf closure conditions and be finitely generated over k. For a D-module P suppose 1⊗_D p=0. Choose a finite witness from step 3 and collect all H-elements appearing as first coordinates, including sums and products in the relations, together with 1. Step 2 provides a finite-dimensional stable V containing them. Put N=span_D(V). Every relation in the witness is valid for N⊗_D P: all its first coordinates belong to N, and N is closed under addition and the D-action.
5. The formal-sum identity of the witness also holds in the free abelian group on N×P, because its map into the free abelian group on H×P is injective: distinct basis pairs stay distinct. Therefore 1⊗p=0 already in N⊗_D P, without assuming injectivity of N⊗_D P→H⊗_D P. Apply the finite_retraction sibling to D and V. Its hypotheses hold, and it gives a D-linear r:N→D with r(1)=1. Applying r⊗id yields 1⊗p=0 in D⊗_D P. The inverse maps d⊗z↦dz and z↦1⊗z identify that tensor product with P, so p=0. Applying the same conclusion to differences proves injectivity of p↦1⊗p in this finitely generated case.
6. Now let D⊂H satisfy the closure conditions without a finite-generation assumption. A finite witness for 1⊗_D p=0 uses finitely many balancing scalars from D. Equip D with the Hopf structure from step 1 and apply the finite_hopf_envelope sibling inside D to those scalars. It supplies a finitely generated k-subalgebra A⊂D containing them and satisfying both closure conditions in D. Its image A'⊂H is isomorphic to A, so is finitely generated. Compatibility of D→H carries the comultiplication-span and antipode conditions to A'.
7. Restrict P to an A'-module. The free abelian group on H×P has not changed, and every balancing scalar of the chosen witness lies in A'. The identical witness therefore proves 1⊗p=0 in H⊗_(A') P. Steps 4–5 give p=0. Thus P→H⊗_D P, p↦1⊗p, is injective for every D-module P and every D satisfying the two closure conditions.
8. Regard K as a K-submodule of H, and let π:H→H/K be the quotient K-linear map. Apply id_H⊗π to the assumed equality x⊗_K1=1⊗_Kx. Since 1∈K, the result is 0=1⊗_Kπ(x). Step 7 with D=K and P=H/K gives π(x)=0. The kernel of π is K, hence x∈K.

## Key steps

1. Construct compatible Hopf structures on the stable subalgebras.
2. Construct finite-dimensional stable spaces containing finite subsets.
3. Use finite tensor-relation witnesses and finite_retraction to prove purity for finitely generated subalgebras.
4. Use finite_hopf_envelope to reduce arbitrary witnesses to the finitely generated case.
5. Apply purity to H/K and the assumed tensor equality.
