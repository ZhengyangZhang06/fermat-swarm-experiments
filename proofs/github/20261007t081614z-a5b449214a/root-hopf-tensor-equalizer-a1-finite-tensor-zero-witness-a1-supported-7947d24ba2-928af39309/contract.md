## Theorem `Submission.p05_ftzw_supported_relations_vanish_a5b449214a`

Let k and D be commutative rings, let D be a k-algebra, and let M and P be D-modules with additive commutative group structures. Fix a k-subalgebra A⊆D and an A-submodule N⊆M, using restricted D-actions throughout. Write G=FreeAbelianGroup(M×P) and [u,z] for its pair generator. Let T(A,N) consist of all [0,z] with z∈P; [u,0] with u∈N; [u+v,z]−[u,z]−[v,z] with u,v∈N and z∈P; [u,z+w]−[u,z]−[u,w] with u∈N and z,w∈P; and [d•u,z]−[u,d•z] with d∈A, u∈N and z∈P. For every m∈N and p∈P, if [m,p] belongs to the additive subgroup of G generated by T(A,N), then m⊗_A p=0 in N⊗_A P.

## Proof

1. Fix all the stated data and let R be the subgroup generated by T(A,N). Finite integer linear combinations of elements of T(A,N) form a subgroup: the empty sum gives zero, concatenation gives addition, and negating coefficients gives additive inverses. This subgroup contains T(A,N), and every subgroup containing T(A,N) contains these combinations. Hence it equals R. The hypothesis therefore gives a finite formal identity [m,p]=∑_{i=1}^n c_i r_i in G, where each c_i is an integer and each r_i has one of the five forms defining T(A,N). Choose such a form and its operands for each r_i.
2. Put G_N=FreeAbelianGroup(N×P). Lift the first coordinates of the chosen relations to N. The zero-left relation lifts to [0,z], and a zero-right relation lifts to [ũ,0]. A first-coordinate additivity relation lifts to [ũ+ṽ,z]−[ũ,z]−[ṽ,z]; a second-coordinate additivity relation lifts to [ũ,z+w]−[ũ,z]−[ũ,w]. The required lifts exist because the chosen u and v belong to N and N is closed under addition and contains zero. For a balancing relation, d∈A gives a=⟨d,hd⟩ in A, and its lift is [a•ũ,z]−[ũ,a•z]. Restricted scalar actions give (a•ũ:M)=d•u and a•z=d•z. Thus all these lifted relations have precisely their chosen original forms after inclusion. Equalities between lifts follow from injectivity of subtype inclusion.
3. The inclusion i:N×P→M×P induces an additive homomorphism J:G_N→G sending every pair generator to the generator of its included pair. If r̃_i denotes the lifted relation from step 2, then J(r̃_i)=r_i. Also J([m,p]) is the original [m,p] in G.
4. The homomorphism J is injective. Indeed, write an element of G_N as its finitely supported integer coefficient function on N×P. Because i is injective, the coefficient of J(h) at i(t) is exactly the coefficient of h at t: no distinct pair contributes at i(t). Thus J(h)=0 implies that every coefficient of h is zero, and hence h=0. Applying this to the difference of any two elements proves injectivity.
5. By step 1 and preservation of finite sums and integer multiples, J([m,p])=J(∑ c_i r̃_i). Injectivity yields [m,p]=∑ c_i r̃_i already in G_N. Evaluate a pair generator [n,z] as n⊗_A z, extending to an additive homomorphism G_N→N⊗_A P. The zero, additivity and A-balancing identities of the tensor product kill all five types of lifted relations from step 2. Therefore the right side evaluates to zero and the left side evaluates to m⊗_A p. This proves the required equality.

## Key steps

1. Express membership in the supported relation subgroup as a finite integer combination.
2. Lift each zero, additivity and balancing relation using the submodule operations and restricted scalar actions.
3. Show that inclusion of pair generators induces an injective free-abelian-group map.
4. Use injectivity to lift the formal identity.
5. Evaluate in the smaller tensor product, where all lifted relations vanish.
