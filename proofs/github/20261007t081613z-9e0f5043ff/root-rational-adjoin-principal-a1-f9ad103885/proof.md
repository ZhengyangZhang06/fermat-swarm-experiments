## Proof

1. Put E = IntermediateField.adjoin K {x}. Evaluation at x embeds K[t] into F: its kernel contains no nonzero polynomial by transcendence. Therefore every nonzero polynomial denominator has nonzero image, and evaluation extends to an injective K-homomorphism φ : K(t) → F. Its image is a field containing K and x, so it contains E. Conversely, E contains every evaluated polynomial fraction, so the image is contained in E. Thus φ gives a K-algebra isomorphism K(t) ≅ E.

2. Explain transport through this isomorphism. Pullback and image under φ give inverse correspondences between valuation subrings of K(t) and E. They preserve containment of K, properness and the principal-ideal-ring property, and hence give a bijection of exactly the project's places. The induced valuation-ring isomorphisms preserve maximal ideals and induce K-algebra isomorphisms of residue fields. They therefore preserve residue finranks. They also preserve normalized orders: choose a uniformizer π in either place ring. The project theorem Place.exists_unit_mul_zpow expresses every nonzero element as uπ^m, and Place.ord_unit_smul_zpow gives order m. The transported unit and uniformizer give the same expression and exponent on the other side. Consequently it suffices to construct the required divisors on K(t), and then reindex them along this place bijection.

3. Recall explicitly the polynomial facts needed below. Euclidean division shows that every ideal of K[t] is principal: divide any element of a nonzero ideal by a nonzero element of least degree, and the remainder must vanish. An irreducible polynomial q is prime, because when q does not divide a the Euclidean algorithm gives uq + va = 1, which implies q divides b whenever q divides ab. Induction on degree factors every nonzero polynomial as a nonzero constant times a finite product of monic irreducibles. Primality gives well-defined, additive multiplicities μ_q. In particular, μ_q(ab) = μ_q(a) + μ_q(b), and the degree of a nonzero polynomial is the sum of its irreducible multiplicities times the corresponding irreducible degrees.

4. For each monic irreducible q, let A_q be the subring of K(t) consisting of fractions a/b with q not dividing b. Factoring out powers of q shows that a nonzero rational function belongs to A_q exactly when μ_q(a) − μ_q(b) is nonnegative. Every nonzero member of A_q is a unit times a nonnegative power of q; exponent zero characterizes units. Either a nonzero rational function or its inverse has nonnegative exponent, so A_q is a valuation subring. It is proper because it omits q⁻¹. Every nonzero ideal is principal: choose an element with the least occurring nonnegative exponent, and it divides every other element of the ideal. Thus A_q is a project place ring, its maximal ideal is generated by q, and its normalized order on a/b is μ_q(a) − μ_q(b).

5. The following overring observation will identify all places. Suppose A is one of the DVRs just described, with uniformizer π, fraction field M, and every nonzero element of M expressible as uπ^m with u an A-unit and m an integer. If a subring V of M contains A and contains z outside A, write z = uπ^(−r), where r > 0. Then z u⁻¹ π^(r−1) = π⁻¹ belongs to V. The same expressions show that A[π⁻¹] = M, so V = M. Consequently any proper overring of A inside M equals A.

6. Let V be the valuation ring of a project place of K(t), and first suppose t belongs to V. Then K[t] is contained in V. The contraction p of the maximal ideal of V to K[t] is prime and nonzero. Indeed, if it were zero, every nonzero polynomial would be a unit of the local ring V, so every polynomial fraction would belong to V, contradicting properness. Since K[t] is a PID, p has a unique monic irreducible generator q. Every polynomial outside (q) is a unit of V, so A_q is contained in V. Step 5 gives V = A_q.

7. Suppose instead that t does not belong to V. The valuation-ring property puts s = t⁻¹ in V, and s is a nonunit, since its inverse is absent. The element s is transcendental over K: multiplying any polynomial relation in t⁻¹ by a sufficiently large power of t would contradict transcendence of t. Thus K[s] is a polynomial ring with fraction field K(t). The contraction of the maximal ideal of V to K[s] contains (s); this is a maximal ideal because K[s]/(s) ≅ K. The contraction is therefore exactly (s). Applying the construction and overring argument of steps 4–6 with variable s and irreducible s gives V = K[s]_(s). This defines the place at infinity. Its ring omits t, whereas every A_q contains t. The contractions (q) distinguish the finite places. Equality of their valuation subrings gives equality of project places by Place.ext. Hence the finite places and infinity classify all project places, without omissions or repetitions.

8. Compute residue degrees at finite places. Reduction modulo q extends from K[t] to A_q because every permitted denominator has nonzero image in the field K[t]/(q). Its kernel is qA_q, and it is surjective, so the residue field is K[t]/(q). If d = deg q, polynomial division shows that the residue classes of 1,t,…,t^(d−1) span. A relation among them would be a polynomial of degree less than d divisible by q, and therefore zero. They form a basis, proving that the project's residue finrank is d.

9. At infinity, reduction modulo s identifies the residue field of K[s]_(s) with K, so its residue degree is 1. For a nonzero polynomial a(t) of degree d and leading coefficient a_d, write a(t) = s^(−d)(a_d + a_(d−1)s + ⋯ + a_0s^d). The parenthesized factor is a unit, since its reduction modulo s is a_d ≠ 0. Therefore ord_∞(a) = −deg a and ord_∞(a/b) = deg b − deg a.

10. Fix g ≠ 0 in K(t), and write g = a/b with a,b nonzero polynomials. Factor a = c∏q q^α_q and b = d∏q q^β_q, with c,d nonzero constants and finitely many nonzero exponents. At a finite place q its order is α_q − β_q. All remaining finite-place orders vanish, and infinity contributes at most one additional support point. Thus v ↦ ord_v(g) has finite support and defines a divisor D.

11. The definition of Divisor.degree and its singleton formula give degree(D) = ∑q (α_q − β_q)deg q + deg b − deg a. The sum is finite. Degree additivity and the factorizations give deg a = ∑q α_q deg q and deg b = ∑q β_q deg q, so this integer is zero. Every coefficient of D is its prescribed order by construction.

12. For any nonzero element of E, apply steps 10–11 to its inverse image under φ and reindex the resulting divisor using the place bijection from step 2. Reindexing preserves finite support; order preservation gives the required coefficients; residue-degree preservation gives degree zero. These witnesses, for all nonzero elements of E, are exactly HasPrincipalDivisors K E.

## Key steps

1. Identify the generated intermediate field with K(t) by transcendental evaluation.
2. Transport project places, normalized orders and residue degrees through that isomorphism.
3. Construct the finite places by irreducible multiplicities and prove the proper-overring argument.
4. Classify every remaining place as the place at infinity.
5. Compute finite residue degrees and orders, and the order and residue degree at infinity.
6. Use polynomial factorization to establish finite support and cancellation of the weighted order sum.
7. Transport the resulting divisors back to the exact intermediate field.
