## Proof

1. Write R = K[T], A = v.toValuationSubring, E(a) = a(x), and ρ : A → κ(v) for the canonical residue homomorphism. Transcendence makes E injective: if E(a) = E(b), then E(a − b) = 0, and the definition of transcendence forces a − b = 0. In particular, nonzero polynomials have nonzero evaluations. Irreducibility implies q ≠ 0 and q is not a unit. Therefore q does not divide 1, since a factorization 1 = q c would make q a unit.
2. For every a ∈ R, the permitted representation E(a) = E(a)/E(1) shows that E(a) belongs to A. Define e(a) to be E(a), regarded as an element of A. The evaluation identities and injectivity of the inclusion A → F show that e preserves zero, one, addition, and multiplication. For c ∈ K, its value on the constant polynomial c is the canonical image of c in A: both elements have image algebraMap K F c in F. Hence e is a K-algebra homomorphism, and its composite with A → F is E.
3. Suppose q does not divide a. Then a ≠ 0, because q divides zero, and E(a) ≠ 0 by injectivity. The membership hypothesis applied to E(1)/E(a) puts E(a)⁻¹ in A. Thus the element of A represented by E(a)⁻¹ is an inverse of e(a), as can be checked after inclusion into F. Consequently e(a) is a unit of A.
4. The element e(q) is not a unit. Indeed, suppose it were a unit and let z ∈ A be its inverse. Since E(q) ≠ 0, the image of z in F is E(q)⁻¹. By the membership hypothesis, choose a,b ∈ R with q not dividing b and E(q)⁻¹ = E(a)/E(b). Here b ≠ 0 and E(b) ≠ 0. Multiplying this equality by E(q)E(b) yields E(b) = E(q)E(a) = E(q a). Injectivity gives b = q a, contradicting q not dividing b.
5. A valuation ring is local, and its nonunits constitute its maximal ideal. Step 4 therefore gives ρ(e(q)) = 0. If q divides a, write a = q s. Then ρ(e(a)) = ρ(e(q))ρ(e(s)) = 0. Conversely, if q does not divide a, step 3 makes e(a) a unit. A unital ring homomorphism sends units to units, and a unit in the nontrivial residue field cannot be zero. Thus ρ(e(a)) ≠ 0. We have proved ρ(e(a)) = 0 if and only if q divides a.
6. Membership of a in ker(ρ ∘ e) means exactly ρ(e(a)) = 0. Membership of a in the principal ideal generated by q means exactly that q divides a: the multiples of q form an ideal containing q and are contained in every ideal containing q. Step 5 identifies these memberships for every a, proving the required kernel equality. Together with the homomorphism and compatibility constructed in step 2, this proves the stated existence.
