## Checked local steps

For a nonzero Laurent coefficient family whose norm weights tend to zero at the
two positive endpoints of a closed annulus, the checks establish finite possible
maximizing exponents, existence of a maximum, finitely many tie radii, and
monotonicity of the maximizing exponent. Over an algebraically closed field,
each positive tie radius is the norm of a unit. Combining these with the earlier
dominant-term argument puts every zero of a summable evaluation at a tie radius.

The convergence check first works in a complete ultrametric field. For an
algebraic extension of a complete field, it works in the finite intermediate
field generated by the evaluation parameter and transfers the sum through its
continuous inclusion. It does not assume the algebraic closure is complete.

For an ultrametric norm, the residue checks construct the valuation ring as the
closed unit ball and identify its maximal ideal by the strict norm inequality. A lifted monic
polynomial and a leading-term valuation argument prove that the residue field
of an algebraically closed valued field is algebraically closed. Normalizing a
decaying coefficient family by a nonzero maximal coefficient gives finite
residue support. Removing its least exponent produces a polynomial with nonzero
constant coefficient and degree equal to the difference of the extreme
exponents. The composed check counts its nonzero roots with multiplicity.

These are residue roots. Their lifting to analytic zeros, with multiplicities,
has not been formalized.
