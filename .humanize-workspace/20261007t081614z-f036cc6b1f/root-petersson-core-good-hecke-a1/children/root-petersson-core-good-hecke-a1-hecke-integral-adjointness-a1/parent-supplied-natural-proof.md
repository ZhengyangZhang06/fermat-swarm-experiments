## Proof

1. Fix M,F,p,hp,hpM,f,g as in the statement, and write Γ=Γ₀(M). The principal subgroup Γ(M), the kernel of reduction modulo M, has finite index because the reduction group is finite. It is contained in Γ, so Γ has finite index. Also −I∈Γ. Thus petersson_integral_core applies to Γ. Since p is prime, p>0; since p does not divide M, gcd(p,M)=1.
2. For a positive-determinant real matrix A=((a,b),(c,d)), use the frozen formula (u|₂A)(z)=det(A)(cz+d)⁻²u(Az). Multiplication of denominators and determinants proves (u|₂A)|₂B=u|₂(AB). A positive scalar matrix tI acts trivially because its determinant t² cancels its denominator factor t². The imaginary-part formula gives P(u|₂A,v|₂A)(z)=P(u,v)(Az), and the real Jacobian gives preservation of μ. These are the weight-two specialization of UpperHalfPlane.petersson_slash and the pinned hyperbolic-measure invariance result.
3. Put α=diag(1,p), H=Γ∩α⁻¹Γα, and K=Γ∩αΓα⁻¹, with intersections taken after embedding the matrices in GL₂(ℚ). For γ=((a,b),(c,d))∈Γ, conjugation gives αγα⁻¹=((a,b/p),(pc,d)), so γ∈H exactly when p divides b. Likewise α⁻¹γα=((a,pb),(c/p,d)), so γ∈K exactly when pM divides c. Both subgroups contain Γ(pM), hence have finite index in SL₂(ℤ), and both contain −I. Directly from their definitions, αHα⁻¹=K.
4. The required slash translates are cusp forms on these subgroups. For any finite-index subgroup Δ of SL₂(ℤ), the coset argument with powers of T gives a positive cusp width at each rational point σ∞. Conversely, a noncentral integer parabolic has either infinity as fixed point or the repeated root (a−d)/(2c) of cz²+(d−a)z−b=0; hence every cusp is rational. Thus Γ,H,K have exactly the rational cusps. If A is a positive-determinant rational matrix and L=Γ∩A⁻¹ΓA has finite index, then f|₂A is L-invariant: AhA⁻¹∈Γ implies (f|₂A)|₂h=f|₂A. It is holomorphic by the slash formula. At a rational cusp σ∞ choose ρ∈SL₂(ℤ) carrying infinity to Aσ∞. Then ρ⁻¹Aσ=((a,b),(0,d)) with a/d>0, and ((f|₂A)|₂σ)(z)=(a/d)(f|₂ρ)((a/d)z+b/d), which tends to zero at infinity. The single-coordinate criterion OnePoint.isZeroAt_iff makes this the full bundled cusp condition. Restriction from Γ to H or K also preserves cusp forms because the cusp sets coincide. Apply this argument to A=α and A=α⁻¹: f|₂α and g are cusp forms on H, while f and g|₂α⁻¹ are cusp forms on K.
5. Compute the right cosets H\Γ. For γ₁,γ₂∈Γ, the upper-right entry of γ₁γ₂⁻¹ is b₁a₂−a₁b₂. Thus Hγ₁=Hγ₂ exactly when their first rows modulo p are proportional. These rows are nonzero because their determinants are one, and ℤ/pℤ is a field. The projective lines with first coordinate nonzero are represented uniquely by (1,j), 0≤j<p; choose t_j=((1,j),(0,1))∈Γ. Bézout gives integers u,v with pu+Mv=1. Set γ*=((p,−v),(M,u)) and β=((1,−v),(M,pu)). Both have determinant one and lower-left entry M, so belong to Γ. Modulo p, the first row of γ* is (0,−v), with v nonzero because Mv=1 modulo p. It therefore represents the remaining projective line. The matrices t_j and γ* form a complete nonrepeating set of right-coset representatives.
6. The assignment Hγ↦Γαγ identifies H\Γ with the left-Γ cosets in ΓαΓ: equality of Γαγ₁ and Γαγ₂ is equivalent to γ₁γ₂⁻¹∈Γ∩α⁻¹Γα. Define A_j=((1,j),(0,p)) and C=diag(p,1). Since αt_j=A_j and αγ*=βC, the double coset is the disjoint union of the left cosets ΓA_j and ΓC. In particular C∈ΓαΓ, so ΓCΓ=ΓαΓ. Since α⁻¹=p⁻¹C and scalar matrices are central, Γα⁻¹Γ=p⁻¹ΓαΓ; its left cosets have representatives p⁻¹A_j and p⁻¹C.
7. For a Γ-invariant function u, let D_αu be the slash sum over left-coset representatives in ΓαΓ. Changing a representative by left multiplication by Γ leaves its summand unchanged, by slash composition and Γ-invariance. Step 6 and the pinned definitions give D_αu=Σ_{0≤j<p}u|₂A_j+u|₂C=ModularForm.heckeT 2 p u. Positive scalars act trivially by step 2, so the inverse double-coset sum D_{α⁻¹}u is the same function. For cusp forms, the coe_heckeTLin_apply identity identifies both sums with the underlying function of the exact bundled operator T=CuspForm.heckeTLin 2 hp hpM.
8. Choose right-coset representatives γ_i for H\Γ and δ_j for K\Γ. Put E_H=⋃_iγ_iF and E_K=⋃_jδ_jF. These are finite measurable unions. To handle all exceptional sets simultaneously, remove the saturation of the null exceptional set for F under the countable group generated by Γ and α. Every element of this group preserves μ, so its complement X is conull and invariant. For z∈X, choose η∈Γ with ηz∈F and write η⁻¹=hγ_i; then h⁻¹z∈γ_iF. This proves H-coverage. If h₁z∈γ_iF and h₂z∈γ_jF, uniqueness for F gives γ_j⁻¹h₂h₁⁻¹γ_i=±I. Since −I∈H, the representative cosets agree, so i=j and h₂=±h₁. Thus E_H satisfies the effective fundamental-domain condition for H. The identical argument with K and δ_j proves it for E_K. It also shows that their displayed pieces are pairwise disjoint outside null sets.
9. By petersson_integral_core applied to H, the function P(f|₂α,g) is integrable on E_H. Restricting to each piece and transporting it to F proves integrability of every summand P(f|₂αγ_i,g) on F. Consequently finite-sum linearity, Γ-invariance of g, and covariance give ∫_F P(D_αf,g)dμ=Σ_i∫_F P(f|₂αγ_i,g)dμ=Σ_i∫_{γ_iF}P(f|₂α,g)dμ=∫_{E_H}P(f|₂α,g)dμ.
10. Slash composition gives g=(g|₂α⁻¹)|₂α. Covariance therefore rewrites the last integrand as P(f,g|₂α⁻¹)(αz). The measure-preserving substitution w=αz yields ∫_{E_H}P(f|₂α,g)dμ=∫_{αE_H}P(f,g|₂α⁻¹)dμ.
11. Conjugation αHα⁻¹=K shows that αE_H satisfies the effective fundamental-domain property for K: transport the representative in H by α, and transport uniqueness by the same conjugation; ±I are unchanged. The image is measurable because α acts by a homeomorphism, and null exceptional sets remain null. By step 4, f and g|₂α⁻¹ are cusp forms on K. Apply the single Core supplied by petersson_integral_core for K to the domains αE_H and E_K. Its two integral identities prove ∫_{αE_H}P(f,g|₂α⁻¹)dμ=∫_{E_K}P(f,g|₂α⁻¹)dμ, and also give absolute integrability on both domains.
12. Split the last integral over the almost-everywhere disjoint pieces δ_jF and transport each piece to F. Since f|₂δ_j=f, covariance gives ∫_{E_K}P(f,g|₂α⁻¹)dμ=Σ_j∫_F P(f,g|₂α⁻¹δ_j)dμ=∫_F P(f,D_{α⁻¹}g)dμ. All summands are integrable by restriction of the integral on E_K and change of variables. The matrices α⁻¹δ_j represent the inverse double coset because its coset correspondence uses Γ∩αΓα⁻¹=K.
13. Combining steps 9–12 proves ∫_F P(D_αf,g)dμ=∫_F P(f,D_{α⁻¹}g)dμ. Substitute the two exact identifications with T from step 7. This is the required equality. Every pairing used the unnormalized integral and each coset occurred once, so no index factor is introduced. The underlying-function identity for heckeTLin holds for every hp and hpM; hence the conclusion has exactly the stated quantification over proof witnesses.
